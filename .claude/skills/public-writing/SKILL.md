# Public Writing

Frameworks for revising or evaluating writing aimed at public audiences (investors, customers, regulators). Apply when rewriting, structuring, or judging clarity, persuasion, reasoning, or tone. Preserve the author's intent and voice unless asked to change it.

## Structure

**SCQA** (openings, section transitions): Situation (concrete current state) → Complication (the friction) → Question (what must be resolved) → Answer (thesis, stated succinctly). Should read as inevitable.

**Minto pyramid**: lead with the governing idea (the answer), then support with grouped arguments that are MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive). Default to 3 pillars: Value (benefit) → Mechanism (how it works) → Controls (safety/boundaries/reliability). Move abstract → concrete.

**Topic/stress position**: known context at the start of a sentence, the new or important information at the end. A paragraph's topic sentence sets expectations; its final sentence delivers the key insight.

## Persuasive & clear writing (Hopkins/Collier direct-response principles)

- Reason-why, not vague claims. Concrete numbers, examples, and mechanisms beat generalities.
- The reader enters mid-stream — don't over-explain.
- Cut weak language: "maybe", "possibly", "in many ways", "sort of", "kind of".
- Adverb test: delete it; if the sentence still means the same thing, leave it out. Keep only adverbs carrying meaning the verb can't (duration, necessary contrast, timing that changes behavior).
- Prefer active voice and visual, tangible phrasing. Lead headlines with tension, stakes, a surprising fact, or a bounded promise.

## Reasoning

Claim → Evidence → Warrant (why the evidence supports the claim) → Boundaries (where it applies/doesn't, assumptions, risks). Apply to paragraphs, sections, product claims, and regulatory statements — make all four parts explicit.

## Precision & regulator-safe language

No absolutes ("always", "cannot fail", "fully compliant"). Use bounded phrasing: "designed to", "aims to", "helps enable", "in testing, we observed…". Separate fact from aspiration — label future work as future work. State explicitly what's excluded, assumptions, risks, and monitoring/audit features. When in doubt, under-claim and over-explain the mechanism.

## Example

Weak: "Our platform revolutionizes data reliability for everyone." — vague, unbounded, no mechanism.

Improved: "Our system improves data reliability by verifying each record through independent proofs before storage. This reduces silent corruption risk for teams handling regulated data." — mechanism-first, specific, bounded.

## Workflow

Identify the central claim → frame with SCQA → group support into 3 MECE pillars → apply topic/stress position at the paragraph level → apply the persuasive-writing rules → run the reasoning chain on major claims → apply the precision/anti-hype pass.
