# Interior Design Principles: Spatial Psychology Reference

Based on neuroscience research on how the brain perceives space. Apply these principles when evaluating a room or recommending changes in visual mockups.

---

## 1. Reclaim the Vertical Plane (Avoid the Single Plane Trap)

**The Problem:** Amateur spaces cluster furniture in a narrow 2–2.5 ft horizontal band. The brain reads this as a flat 2D image — the "dollhouse effect."

**The Fix:** Move the eye in a **W or M pattern** across varying heights.

**Tactics:**
- **Curtain height:** Mount rods **2–4 inches below the ceiling line** (not on the window frame). The brain infers a grander floor-to-ceiling window.
- **Vertical lofting:** Use fluted wall panels or tall bookcases. Vertical lines force the eye upward, raising perceived ceiling height.

---

## 2. Create Horizontal Depth (Float Your Furniture)

**The Problem:** Furniture flush against walls merges with architecture, flattening the space.

**The Fix:** Pull sofas and main seating **6 inches from the wall** to create a shadow line that signals 3D volume.

**Landscape Layering Model** — treat the room like a painting:
| Layer | Elements |
|---|---|
| Background | Walls, curtains, wall art |
| Middle Ground | Main furniture anchor (sofa, bed) |
| Foreground | Coffee table or end bench |

All three layers must be present for the brain to read depth.

---

## 3. Master Contrast (Ditch "The Big Light")

**The Problem:** A single overhead light floods with uniform brightness, washing out contrast and making objects look like flat cardboard cutouts.

**The Fix:** Directional light at a **45° angle** (side lighting/grazing) creates a **terminator line** — the boundary where light meets shadow — which helps the brain calculate volume and texture.

**3 Lighting Layers (all required):**
1. **Mood (Ambient):** Soft baseline glow — sets overall warmth
2. **Task:** Focused lighting for reading, cooking, work
3. **Accent:** Directional lights highlighting specific features or textures

---

## 4. Asymmetrical Styling (The 3-5-7 Rule)

**The Problem:** Symmetrical pairings (2s, 4s, 6s) make spaces feel stiff and catalog-like.

**The Fix:** Arrange decor in **odd numbers** using Vignette Triangles.

**Vignette Triangle composition:**
| Height | Example |
|---|---|
| Tall (vertical peak) | Vase of flowers, tall candle |
| Medium | Ceramic jug, sculpture |
| Flat/Low | Stacked books, marble tray |

For long consoles/surfaces: scale up to 5 or 7 items with **intentional gaps of negative space** between groups.

---

## 5. Avoid the Sanitization Trap (The 80/20 Rule)

**The Problem:** Over-coordinating every element (all-beige, all mid-century, pure showroom style) strips out personality and feels sterile.

**The 80/20 Rule of Curation:**
- **80% Rules & Structure:** Clean lines, cohesive color palette, correct lighting layers — the framework that makes the space readable.
- **20% Uncorrected Friction:** Imperfect, personal, vintage, or slightly clashing pieces — a worn leather chair, flea-market lamp, 19th-century chest — that add **historical weight and proof of life**.

The 20% friction is what makes a space feel inhabited, not staged.

---

## Quick-Reference Checklist

Before finalizing any recommendation, verify:
- [ ] Eye moves in W/M pattern across multiple heights
- [ ] Curtain rods mounted 2–4 inches below ceiling
- [ ] Main seating floated 6 inches from walls
- [ ] All three landscape layers present (background / middle / foreground)
- [ ] No single overhead "big light" — at least 2 of 3 lighting layers used
- [ ] Decor arranged in odd-number groupings (3, 5, or 7)
- [ ] At least one "friction" piece (vintage, personal, imperfect) in the 20%
